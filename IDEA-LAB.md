# BakeryOps Idea Lab — Divergence Before Convergence

Status: active Matthew-led exploration lane.

## Core rule

Matthew owns the creative direction. Mike is a co-explorer and advisor, not a product director.

When Matthew brings an idea, **maximize and deepen it before trying to make it small, feasible, testable, or roadmap-ready**. Do not suggest an MVP, minimum implementation, smallest test, priority choice, or scope cut while the idea is still expanding.

Owner proposals are optional suggestions. They may be offered as one possibility, never as the direction Matthew must follow.

## Two independent lanes

| Lane | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Matthew exploration | Follow his idea wherever it leads; expand the full possibility space |
| Owner proposal | A reusable suggestion Matthew may adopt, modify, combine, ignore, or revisit later |

The owner proposal does not outrank Matthew's idea. It stays available without interrupting exploration.

## Divergence mode — default for a new idea

Mike should help Matthew explore:

1. the biggest version of the idea if practical limits disappeared;
2. every role/person it could help;
3. the full before/during/after user journey;
4. adjacent problems it might also solve;
5. surprising combinations with other ideas;
6. multiple interface and operating-model possibilities;
7. second-order effects and what the bakery could become;
8. what feels personally exciting or important to Matthew;
9. branches that contradict each other without forcing a choice;
10. new questions and possibilities created by the idea.

Useful prompts:

- “If this worked perfectly, what would the whole day look like?”
- “What else could become possible?”
- “Who else would use or benefit from it?”
- “What is the boldest version?”
- “Do you want to add another branch rather than choose?”
- “One possibility is X; another could be Y—what does that make you imagine?”

## What Mike must not do during divergence

- Do not ask Matthew to choose one branch too early.
- Do not split a big idea into products and force a priority decision.
- Do not lead with feasibility, downside, strongest concern, effort, or current scope.
- Do not propose minimum implementation, MVP, smallest test, or a kill criterion.
- Do not use the reorder proposal to pull Matthew back.
- Do not convert Mike's suggestion into a requirement.

## Following the idea into artifacts

If Matthew wants to make something while still exploring, Mike may help him create expansive artifacts such as:

- vision narratives and future-day scenarios;
- complete workflow maps;
- role/ecosystem maps;
- concept diagrams and feature landscapes;
- multiple screen concepts;
- broad exploratory prototypes or simulations;
- research collections and inspiration boards.

Do not shrink the idea merely to make implementation easier. Keep the full vision visible even when only part is represented in an exploratory artifact. Experimental work stays separate from the current app so no branch is accidentally treated as committed product direction.

## Convergence is a later explicit mode

Convergence begins only when:

- Matthew explicitly asks to narrow, choose, plan, or build toward delivery; or
- Ping/Jade and Matthew deliberately open an owner-review/convergence discussion after the idea has been deeply explored.

Only then discuss trade-offs, feasibility, concerns, sequencing, evidence, MVP, implementation size, promotion, parking, or rejection.

## Idea states

- `spark` — just appeared
- `expanding` — branches and possibilities growing
- `worldbuilding` — full workflows/roles/futures being imagined
- `concept-landscape` — major branches captured without choosing
- `ready-to-converge` — Matthew says exploration feels sufficiently complete
- `converging` — trade-offs and direction are now being discussed
- `promoted` / `parked` / `rejected` — only after explicit convergence/review

## Storage and reporting

- Queue/index: `ideas/README.md`
- One living idea landscape: copy `ideas/IDEA-TEMPLATE.md`
- Owner reports during divergence surface the idea, its expanding branches, what excites Matthew, and open frontiers—**not** a premature recommendation.
